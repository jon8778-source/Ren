# Research / factual claims review (9 November 2026)

This is a FICTIONAL educational story, not a reported account of someone's finances. All character events, day numbers, bills, balance changes and savings transfers are invented illustrative figures. Do not present them as typical bills, national averages, or empirically sourced statistics.

## Supporting sources

1. US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, **An essential guide to building an emergency fund**: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/an-essential-guide-to-building-an-emergency-fund/
   - An emergency fund is a reserve for unplanned costs such as car, home and medical repairs or loss of income.
   - Consistent saving and automatic transfers may help build savings, while watching checking balances to avoid overdraft fees.
   - Funds should be accessible and safe; an emergency withdrawal is an intended use and can be rebuilt.
   - Credit can impose interest and fees; an emergency cushion may reduce reliance on borrowing.

2. US Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, **Deposit Insurance**: https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/
   - Insured checking and savings deposits at qualifying institutions are protected subject to the relevant FDIC limits and ownership rules.
   - Stocks are NOT FDIC-insured. (Avoid treating invested emergency reserves as instant risk-free cash.)

3. US FDIC, **Preparing for Tax Season?** (updated May 28, 2026): https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-resource-center/2025-02/preparing-tax-season
   - The appropriate emergency reserve depends on income, expenses, and household circumstances. Three to six months of expenses is a broad conventional reference, not a requirement for every viewer and not presented as a guarantee in the script.

## Editorial checks

- Do not claim a six-month buffer or any particular sum is universally necessary.
- Do not claim all emergencies can be paid from a small fund. Maya's fictional $2,600 fund is NOT a full income-replacement buffer.
- Avoid describing $760/$390/$1,050 as average or typical costs.
- Savings arithmetic does not account for interest, taxes, bank fees, changes in income, prior withdrawals or price increases. This is intentionally simplified, visible through `production/assumptions.csv`.
- Do not claim Maya definitely avoided all financial consequences; only the consequences narrated in her fictional scenario.
- Disclaimers at beginning and end: "Educational only, not financial advice"; "Illustrative example" beside financial illustrations.
- Before publishing, recheck current US consumer guidance and all numbers. If source or claim changes, revise the script and obtain fresh owner approval.
