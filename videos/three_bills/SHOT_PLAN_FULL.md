Status: PLAN for owner review. Times are the script's editorial guides (6:02 target); they will be re-cut to the measured Qwen audio (the pilot narration measures about 0.34 s/word, i.e. roughly
5:40-6:00 for the full 937 words). The pilot (0:00-~1:00) is built; everything below the pilot line is unbuilt.

Legend: REN = genuine library clip (Ren left in the sage office, graphics right, max 5.5 s). MAYA = approved four-pose cut-out on an approved plate (grounded with ren_stage, camera moves
are real framing changes). PROP = diegetic object close-up with code-drawn figures (phone, invoice, pipe, calendar, card). GFX = code graphic that is NOT attached to an object or a person
(the thing to limit). Caps: REN/MAYA action 5.5 s, story still 2.5 s, graphic 3.5 s.

## What the assets can and cannot do (the constraint behind the plan)
- Maya has FOUR standing poses only (neutral, surprised with envelope, worried with phone, relief hugging phone). No walking, sitting, pointing or hand-over-hand actions exist; any "action" is
  a pose change, a camera move, a prop interaction or Maya entering/leaving by cut. Do not claim actor animation. A fifth/sixth pose (reach to the sink, hand over mouth) would widen the range: see gaps.
- Ren has 33 genuine clips; most useful here: present_palm_left (mirrored), point_right, shrug, thinking, nod_yes, open_arms, worried (gritted teeth ~2 s: avoid), hold_phone, clipboard_tick, count_fingers,
  thumbs_up (toothy grin: avoid), explain_hands (window 9-28 s, mouth closed).
- Approved plates: parking lot with car, porch, corporate office, Ren's sage office. Provisional (NOT approved, schematic vector style that does not match): kitchen, dental. Both would clash with the
  painterly plates; plan uses props on a veil of the approved plates instead, unless the owner approves the vector plates.

## Ren returns (the 30-45 s rule)
Planned returns at 0:00, 0:12, 0:29, 0:59, 1:00 (continuing), 1:17, 1:42, 1:58, 2:22, 2:39, 3:03, 3:18, 3:40, 4:00, 4:27, 4:43, 4:56, 5:08, 5:31, 5:48. Longest gap about 33 s. No return is forced into a meaningful
action (the car-key and pipe close-ups end before Ren returns). 20 returns is the maximum; some will merge when the audio is cut.

## Risk: extended repetitive information-card sequences
The earlier 401(k) film (the baseline) had a middle of about 90-100 s with graphics on a blurred backdrop back to back (budget lines, vesting timeline, match formula, true-up, pay stub / statement),
roughly 2:00-3:30 in the 5:42 cut. The same risk exists here in two places:
1. **4:41-5:31, four principles (about 50 s):** the script is four consecutive "First/Second/Third/Fourth" instructions. Planned mitigation, one scene per principle, each different in setting and object:
   (1) first target: Maya on the porch with the pipe, key and invoice props as small diegetic icons beside her, Ren on "think about the expense that would cause the most trouble"; (2) automatic transfer: a phone
   PROP with the bank app and a weekly transfer toggle, Ren clipboard_tick; (3) predictable bills: three labelled envelopes (insurance, gifts, maintenance) as PROPs on the porch bench, Maya neutral;
   (4) refill: the weekly transfer restarting on the phone, Maya relief. Ren between principles. Never more than two graphics-only shots in a row (checked by the QA script).
2. **3:57-4:41 (the reframe):** the "she kept her car working / dental care / stopped the leak" run is three prop close-ups; interleave with Ren and Maya relief and keep each prop to about 2 s.
Planned control: the shot list is generated from a table with a type per shot and the QA script fails any run of 3 or more consecutive GRAPHIC/PROP-without-character shots, any Ren gap over 45 s,
and any graphic longer than 3.5 s.
