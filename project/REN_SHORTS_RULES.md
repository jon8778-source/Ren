# Ren Shorts production rules — story-first v2
Status: owner-approved creative direction, 7 November 2026.
Scope: vertical YouTube Shorts. Supplement project/REN_VIDEO_RULES.md; preserve existing long-form rules.

## Creative reference and priority
- Primary story-first reference: owner's previously generated 60-second Short `2404.mp4` (Alex deciding whether to book a trip). This file may exist only in the owner's ChatGPT attachments; do not claim it is stored in this repository or invent a path.
- Retain the technical lessons from health insurance prototype revision 6, but do not use its presenter-and-document-heavy structure as the creative template.
- Tell a complete miniature story: relatable choice, tension, decision, payoff and one useful financial insight. Ren is the recognisable host/narrator, not automatically the protagonist.
- Let Alex, Maya or another approved supporting character live the story when that improves the narrative. Never force supporting characters into a story that does not need them.

## Visual direction
- Target balance for planning: roughly 60% illustrated story scenes, 25% Ren host, 15% integrated graphics. These are guidelines, not frame quotas; story clarity overrides percentages.
- Prefer character actions and concrete settings: home, phone, workplace, café, shop, outdoors, doctor's office, pharmacy, etc. Only use locations justified by the script.
- Vary close-ups, over-the-shoulder views, medium shots and environmental cutaways. Change the visual meaning or framing roughly every 2–4 seconds where useful; no frantic cutting merely to hit a target.
- Avoid repeated Ren presenter poses, long static holds, looping gestures, floating document cards, checklist walls, template-looking charts and slideshow transitions.
- Show actions rather than labels: tapping to book, reading a bill, choosing between options, enjoying the outcome.
- Use an emotional payoff and a visually legible final takeaway. Avoid promising guaranteed savings or misleading financial results.

## Art and character lock
- Use approved Ren v3 face, black hair, round glasses, navy overshirt over white shirt, proportions and flat 2D visual language. Approved supporting cast must belong to the same visual world.
- Reuse approved library assets first. Match line weight, palette, facial proportions and shading across Ren, Alex, Maya, objects and environments.
- No generic stock-photo or AI-looking cutaways that clash with the art. Photography is not the default. New major character designs or stylistic changes require owner approval.
- Before rendering, inventory existing assets and identify missing props, expressions, poses and environments. Do not silently substitute off-model artwork.

## Portrait-first composition
- Native 1080×1920, 9:16; no persistent blue border, decorative frame, letterbox or empty sidebars.
- Fill the screen with purposeful artwork. Make faces, actions and key figures large enough for phone viewing.
- Keep captions short, legible and away from YouTube Shorts interface overlays. Avoid small dialogue cards.
- Embed essential figures in the story world when possible (phone, receipt, bill, screen), but render numerical labels programmatically for accuracy.
- Never sacrifice readability for an elaborate graphic.

## Voice and editorial integrity
- Use the approved British male Qwen voice per REN_VIDEO_RULES.md; reuse approved narration for visual-only edits. Never substitute another TTS service without permission.
- Preserve natural pacing, no arbitrary speed changes, padding or silent gaps. Stop and report missing Qwen assets or quota blockers.
- Use the verified source script and financial assumptions. Label illustrative values; distinguish premiums from total costs, and savings from spending. Check every on-screen figure.

## Production gates
1. Review script for story arc, visual hooks and factual accuracy.
2. Provide a scene-by-scene storyboard and asset inventory. Request approval for major new character art.
3. Render a 15-second portrait prototype including voice and at least three meaningful visual compositions. Stop for explicit owner approval.
4. Once approved, reuse the opening and render remaining scenes. Do not repeatedly regenerate approved artwork or narration.
5. Review the full Short for narrative continuity, scene variety, style consistency, mobile legibility, sound and arithmetic before final delivery.

## QA and deliverables
- Automated: aspect ratio, resolution, duration, decoding, audio presence/continuity, missing assets, out-of-bounds text and repeated scene identifiers where detectable.
- Human visual review: on-model faces, credible character interactions, genuinely varied environments, readable graphics, non-repetitive pacing and emotional payoff.
- Report limitations honestly; metadata checks alone do not establish professional quality.
- Deliver preview, final MP4 after approval, concise asset/change summary and QA report. Do not publish without permission.
- Keep work on a production/feature branch; do not modify main without explicit instruction.
