# Two Neighbors, Same $1,000 Bonus. Only One Was Happier a Year Later

## hook
[h01] Meet Max and Lena.
[h02] Same street. Same salary.
[h03] And on the same Friday, they both open an envelope with a one thousand dollar bonus inside. {bill}
[h04] A year from now, one of them will barely remember what they spent it on.
[h05] The other will still be feeling the benefit every single week.
[h06] Same money. Completely different results.
[h07] So what did Lena know that Max didn't?

## meet
[m01] Max and Lena are both thirty. {max30.age}
[m02] They both earn sixty thousand dollars a year, and they've lived next door to each other for three years. {max30.gross_salary, max30.years_next_door}
[m03] They borrow each other's ladders, they wave over the fence, and they both work hard.
[m04] Neither of them is bad with money.
[m05] Quick note: Max and Lena are made up, and the numbers are just examples, not financial advice.

## max
[x01] Max knows exactly what he wants.
[x02] That same weekend, he walks out of the store with a brand new sixty-five inch TV for seven hundred dollars. {max30.tv_inches, max30.tv}
[x03] With the last three hundred, he buys a pair of sneakers he's been eyeing for months. {max30.sneakers}
[x04] And honestly? The first week is great.
[x05] Movie night feels like being at the theater.
[x06] The sneakers get compliments at work.
[x07] But by week three, something changes. {max30.fade_week}
[x08] The TV is just... the TV. The sneakers are just shoes.
[x09] Nothing went wrong.
[x10] Max still owns everything he bought.
[x11] It just stopped feeling special.

## lena
[l01] Lena splits her bonus three ways. {=3}
[l02] Four hundred dollars goes to a cleaner. {lena30.cleaner}
[l03] Four visits, about two and a half hours each. {lena30.cleaner_visits, lena30.hours_per_visit}
[l04] That's ten hours of her weekends handed back. {lena30.hours_back}
[l05] Four hundred and fifty dollars goes to a weekend away with her two oldest friends, booked three months ahead. {lena30.trip, lena30.trip_friends, lena30.trip_months_ahead}
[l06] And the last one hundred and fifty pays for a cooking class with her partner. {lena30.cooking_class}
[l07] None of it sits on a shelf.
[l08] Some of it she won't even enjoy for months.
[l09] But the moment she books that trip, something starts happening.

## later
[y01] Fast forward one year. {lena30.years_later}
[y02] Max's TV is still on the wall.
[y03] He watches it most nights, but he doesn't really notice it anymore.
[y04] Next door, Lena is still laughing about the weekend away.
[y05] She still makes the curry she learned at the cooking class.
[y06] And those ten hours? She spent them in the park, not scrubbing the bathroom. {lena30.hours_back}
[y07] Same thousand dollars. Very different year.

## why
[w01] So what happened?
[w03] When we buy something new, we get a happiness boost.
[w04] But we get used to it fast, and the feeling fades back to normal.
[w05] Experiences work differently.
[w06] You look forward to them before, you enjoy them during, and you get to relive them afterward.
[w07] Max got one spike. Lena got three. {max30.spikes, lena30.spikes}
[w08] So here's how to spend like Lena.

## rules
[r01] Rule one: buy experiences over things. {=1}
[r02] Experiences become part of our story, and we don't compare them as much.
[r03] A new phone gets outdated. A great trip doesn't.
[r04] Rule two: buy back your time. {=2}
[r05] If there's a task you hate and you can afford to hand it off, that can be money well spent.
[r06] Ask yourself: what job drains me the most, and what would it cost to skip it?
[r07] Rule three: spend on other people. {=3}
[r08] Lena's weekend away and her cooking class were really about time with the people she cares about.
[r09] It doesn't have to be big. A coffee, a dinner, a shared day out.
[r10] Rule four: pay now, enjoy later. {=4}
[r11] Looking forward to an experience can be a big part of the fun.
[r12] Book things ahead, and you get weeks of excitement before the day even arrives.
[r13] Rule five: make treats rare so they stay special. {lena30.rules}
[r14] A fancy coffee every day stops feeling like a treat.
[r15] Make it a Friday thing, and suddenly it's something to look forward to again.

## side
[s01] Let's look at them side by side.
[s02] Max spent one thousand dollars, and a year later, he owns a TV and some worn-in sneakers. {bill}
[s03] Lena spent the same one thousand dollars, and she got ten free hours, a weekend she'll never forget, a new skill, and time with the people she loves. {bill, lena30.hours_back}
[s04] Max didn't waste his money. Lena just made it go further.
