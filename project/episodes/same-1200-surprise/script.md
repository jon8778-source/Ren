## hook
[h01] One Tuesday, the same envelope lands in five mailboxes. {=5}
[h02] Inside is a surprise dental bill for one thousand two hundred dollars. {bill}
[h03] Same bill, same month, but five very different paychecks. {=5}
[h04] Sam takes home two thousand four hundred dollars a month. {sam.income}
[h05] Richard takes home twenty-two thousand. {richard.income}
[h06] So Richard handles it best, right?
[h07] Not so fast. Income isn't what decides who stays calm this month.
[h08] Something else does, and by the end, you'll spot it in your own budget.

## rules
[r01] Here's the experiment.
[r02] Five people, the same month, and the same one thousand two hundred dollar bill. {=5, bill}
[r03] They're made-up people with simplified budgets, and every number is an example.
[r04] For each of them, we'll look at what comes in, what goes out, and what's left.
[r05] Keep score as we go: who stays calm, and who doesn't?

## level1
[l01] Level one: meet Sam. {=1}
[l02] Sam is twenty-four, works in retail, and brings home two thousand four hundred dollars a month. {=24, sam.income}
[l03] Rent on a shared apartment, utilities, groceries, the bus and health insurance take two thousand one hundred seventy. {sam.fixed_total}
[l04] Then come the little leaks: eighty dollars of delivery fees and a late fee. {sam.leaks_total}
[l05] That leaves Sam one hundred fifty dollars a month, with just two hundred in savings. {sam.left_over, sam.savings}
[l06] Then, on Tuesday, the envelope arrives.
[l07] One thousand two hundred dollars, due in thirty days. {bill, bill_due_days}
[l08] Sam can't cover it, so Sam does something simple, and asks.
[l09] The dentist's office agrees to a payment plan.
[l10] Two hundred from savings now, then one hundred dollars a month for ten months. {sam.savings, sam.plan_monthly, sam.plan_months}
[l11] It fits inside Sam's one hundred fifty dollar gap, just. {sam.left_over}
[l12] It's tight, but Sam isn't panicking, because Sam has a plan.
[l13] Sam's habit: turning on autopay, so those late fees stop for good.

## level2
[n01] Level two: meet Nina. {=2}
[n02] Nina works in an office and takes home three thousand eight hundred a month. {nina.income}
[n03] Rent, her car, groceries, bills and a student loan take two thousand nine hundred ninety. {nina.fixed_total}
[n04] Takeout and subscriptions take another one hundred eighty-four. {nina.leaks_total}
[n05] That leaves her six hundred twenty-six a month, and over time she's saved two thousand. {nina.left_over, nina.savings}
[n06] The bill arrives, and her savings cover all of it, with eight hundred to spare. {nina.savings_after_bill}
[n07] But even Nina has a surprise.
[n08] Checking her statement, she finds sixty-four dollars a month of subscriptions she forgot she had. {nina.leak:forgotten_subscriptions}
[n09] She cancels them that night, and that money goes straight back into her buffer.
[n10] Nina's habit: a ten-minute leak check at the start of every month. {=10}
[n11] Remember Nina. And stick around, because the biggest paycheck in this video hides the biggest surprise.

## level3
[c01] Level three: meet Claire. {=3}
[c02] Claire got a promotion last year, and now takes home five thousand six hundred a month. {claire.income}
[c03] Her fixed costs are three thousand nine hundred thirty, including the minimum on a credit card. {claire.fixed_total}
[c04] But look at the leaks: one thousand five hundred a month on dining out, shopping and subscriptions. {claire.leaks_total}
[c05] When her pay went up, her spending quietly went up with it.
[c06] So she has just one hundred seventy left each month, and six hundred saved. {claire.left_over, claire.savings}
[c07] The bill arrives, and the six hundred her savings can't cover goes on the credit card. {claire.bill_shortfall}
[c08] Now that bill grows with interest, on top of the balance she already had.
[c09] Claire earns more than double what Sam does, but her monthly gap is only twenty dollars bigger. {=20}
[c10] Claire's habit: the next time her pay goes up, half the raise goes straight to savings.

## level4
[m01] Level four: meet Marcus. {=4}
[m02] Marcus works in tech and takes home nine thousand five hundred a month. {marcus.income}
[m03] But big-city rent, a car lease, childcare and student loans add up to seven thousand eight hundred eighty. {marcus.fixed_total}
[m04] That's eighty-three cents of every dollar, gone before the month even starts. {marcus.fixed_share_pct}
[m05] With five hundred twenty in leaks, he has one thousand one hundred left, and one thousand five hundred saved. {marcus.leaks_total, marcus.left_over, marcus.savings}
[m06] The bill arrives, and his savings cover it.
[m07] But that leaves just three hundred in the bank, with costs this high. {marcus.savings_after_bill}
[m08] One more surprise, and Marcus is in trouble.
[m09] A car repair or a childcare bill next month would hit an almost empty account.
[m10] Marcus's habit: rebuilding that buffer first, before taking on anything new.

## level5
[x01] Level five: meet Richard. {=5}
[x02] Richard takes home twenty-two thousand a month, more than nine times what Sam earns. {richard.income, richard.income_multiple_vs_level1}
[x03] His mortgage, two car payments, private school and a housekeeper add up to eighteen thousand one hundred fifty. {=2, richard.fixed_total}
[x04] And here's the biggest surprise in this video.
[x05] Richard's leaks are two thousand nine hundred fifty a month. {richard.leaks_total}
[x06] His leaks alone are bigger than Sam's entire paycheck.
[x07] That leaves nine hundred a month, and one thousand saved. {richard.left_over, richard.savings}
[x08] The bill arrives, and Richard is the one who panics.
[x09] Paying it takes all his savings, plus two hundred more, and leaves him with no buffer at all. {richard.bill_shortfall}
[x10] Earning nine times more than Sam didn't buy him a calmer month. {=9}
[x11] Richard's habit, starting now: a monthly leak check, just like Nina's.

## twist
[t01] So who stayed calmest?
[t02] Not Richard, with the biggest paycheck. It was Nina.
[t03] Income didn't decide who coped. The gap between earning and spending did, and a small buffer.
[t04] Sam had a plan. Nina had a buffer. Claire had a credit card. Marcus had a thin cushion. And Richard had nothing left.
[t05] Here are three takeaways. {=3}
[t06] One: know your monthly gap, what's left after everything goes out. {=1}
[t07] Two: build a starter buffer, even a small one. {=2}
[t08] Three: hunt your leaks every month, because they quietly widen or close that gap. {=3}
