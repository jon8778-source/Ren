# The $3,000 Trip vs $51,000 at 70: Which Is Worth More?

## hook
[h01] Here's a question that sounds easy.
[h02] You're twenty-eight. {you.start_age}
[h03] You've got three thousand dollars. {you.lump}
[h04] You can take the trip you've talked about for years… or invest it.
[h05] At seven percent a year, that three thousand becomes about fifty-one thousand dollars by the time you're seventy. {you.rate_pct, you.lump, you.about_51k, you.end_age}
[h06] So the smart move is obvious, right?
[h07] Well… not quite.
[h08] Because there's something that money can't buy back.

## memory
[m01] There's an idea from a book called Die With Zero by Bill Perkins. {=0}
[m02] He calls it the memory dividend.
[m03] When you pay for an experience, you don't just get the experience.
[m04] You get the memory, and that memory keeps paying out.
[m05] Every time you tell the story.
[m06] Every time a song takes you back to that beach.
[m07] Every time a friend says "remember when…"
[m08] The trip lasts two weeks. {you.trip_weeks}
[m09] The dividend lasts forty years. {you.dividend_years}
[m10] And just like interest, the earlier you invest in a memory, the longer it pays you back.

## catch
[c01] Now let's look at that fifty-one thousand dollars at seventy. {you.about_51k, you.end_age}
[c02] It's real money.
[c03] It could pay for a cruise, help with bills, or become a gift for the grandkids.
[c04] All good things.
[c05] But it can't buy you a backpacking trip with twenty-eight-year-old knees. {you.start_age}
[c06] It can't buy a week with friends who've since moved away, or with kids who've since grown up.
[c07] Your money can keep growing as you get older.
[c08] Your health and energy don't always keep up.
[c09] And some experiences quietly expire.
[c10] That's not because you can't afford them.
[c11] It's because you've aged out of them.

## buckets
[b01] So how do you avoid that?
[b02] Perkins suggests something called time buckets, and it's simple.
[b03] Grab a sheet of paper and draw five boxes: your thirties, forties, fifties, sixties and seventies. {you.boxes}
[b04] Fill them with what you want to do: trips, hobbies, skills, time with people.
[b05] Then you'll notice something.
[b06] Some things fit anywhere.
[b07] A cooking class works at thirty or seventy. {=30, =70}
[b08] Others only fit in one or two boxes, like skiing hard, backpacking, or building sandcastles with a five-year-old. {=1, =2, you.kid_age}
[b09] Those are the ones with an expiry date.
[b10] Fund those first.

## give
[g01] The same idea applies to giving.
[g02] If you plan to leave money to your kids, that's lovely.
[g03] But think about the timing.
[g04] If you have kids around twenty-eight and live to eighty-eight, they inherit at sixty. {you.kids_at, you.live_to, you.inherit_age}
[g05] Ten thousand dollars at sixty is nice. {you.gift, you.inherit_age}
[g06] Ten thousand dollars at thirty could be a first home deposit, or breathing room with a new baby. {you.gift, you.gift_early_age}
[g07] It's the same money with a very different impact.
[g08] So if you can afford to, consider giving some of it while it makes the biggest difference, and while you're around to see it.

## guard
[r01] Now, before anyone books a flight on a credit card: this is not "spend it all."
[r02] Die With Zero only works if you won't run out. {=0}
[r03] So, foundations first.
[r04] That means an emergency fund, no high-interest debt, and retirement savings on track.
[r05] Second, know your "enough" number.
[r06] That's roughly what you'll need for the life you want later.
[r07] Once you're on track for it, extra savings stop being safety and start being permission.
[r08] Third, give your buckets a budget: a set amount each month for experiences, so you spend on purpose, not on impulse.
[r09] And remember, plenty of the best memories are cheap.
[r10] A camping weekend. A day at the beach. A long dinner with friends.

## close

[e02] If your foundations are solid, the trip might be the better investment.
[e03] Not on a spreadsheet, but in your life.
[e04] Save on purpose. Spend on purpose.
[e05] The goal was never to die with the most money.
[e06] It was to live the best life your money could buy.
