For four years, I renewed the same health plan. One click. Same plan. Done. Then I compared my options. One plan: two thousand five hundred and twenty dollars a year in premiums. Another: just eight hundred and forty. But here's the catch. Cheaper premiums didn't automatically mean a better deal.

I asked the group chat. Alex said, “Pick the best.” Danny said, “Pick the cheapest.”

If you're expecting a baby, planning surgery, seeing specialists regularly or taking expensive medication, the PPO could be much better. Check both networks, your drug coverage, the exact deductible and the out-of-pocket limits. Remember that high expenses can arrive before your HSA has much money in it.

Back at my laptop, I didn't click “Keep my current plan.” I checked my own numbers and made a deliberate choice.

I learned to ask: what's the bad year, what's the real year, and where would the difference go? Forty minutes once a year. That's a better habit than four seconds of autopilot.
