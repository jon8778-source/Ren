## hook
[h01] This is Frank.
[h02] Frank just died at eighty-five, with more than two million dollars in the bank. {frank.end_age, =2000000}
[h03] On his desk is a travel brochure for Italy.
[h04] He picked it up when he was thirty-two. {=32}
[h05] He never went.
[h06] Frank did everything right with money.
[h07] So why does this story feel so wrong?
[h08] Today: three friends, one income, and three very different lives. {=3, =1, =3}
[h09] Only one of them gets it right. {=1}

## meet
[m01] Meet Frank, Yara and Bea.
[m02] They're twenty-five, they all earn sixty thousand dollars a year, and they all start work in the same month. {frank.start_age, frank.gross_salary}
[m03] Same job, same money.
[m04] The only difference is what they do with it.
[m05] Quick note: these people are made up, and the numbers are examples, not advice.

## frank
[f01] Let's start with Frank.
[f02] Frank is a saving machine.
[f03] He builds an emergency fund in his first year.
[f04] He never carries credit card debt.
[f05] He invests every month, automatically.
[f06] Honestly? That's great.
[f07] Do those three things and you're already off to a strong start. {=3}
[f08] But Frank doesn't stop there.
[f09] Frank saves half of everything he takes home.
[f10] He eats the cheapest meals, skips every wedding abroad, and when his friends plan a trip, he says the same thing every time: 'Maybe next year.'

## nextyear
[y01] At thirty-two, Frank picks up that brochure for Italy. {=32}
[y02] Next year.
[y03] At forty, his daughter asks him to come to a concert. {=40}
[y04] Too expensive. Next year.
[y05] At fifty, his knees start to complain, and the hiking trip he always wanted feels harder. {=50}
[y06] Next year.
[y07] Here's what Frank never saw.
[y08] Look at this chart.
[y09] This line is Frank's money. It goes up and up.
[y10] But this line is his health and energy, and it slowly comes down.
[y11] Some experiences have an expiry date.
[y12] You can't take your kids to the park when they're thirty. {=30}
[y13] You can't backpack across Italy at seventy-five the way you could at thirty-five. {=75, =35}
[y14] Waiting doesn't just cost money. It costs moments.

## retire
[r01] Frank retires at sixty-five as a multi-millionaire. {frank.retire_age}
[r02] And then he keeps living like he's broke, because forty years of habit is hard to switch off. {=40}
[r03] Research on retirees suggests this is surprisingly common: many people with large savings spend very little of them, even nearly two decades into retirement. {=2}
[r04] Frank was never poor. He just never let himself be rich.
[r05] And hold that thought, because the person who gets this right doesn't save the most, or spend the most.

## yara
[a01] So the answer is to spend it all, right?
[a02] Meet Yara.
[a03] Yara's motto is 'you only live once.'
[a04] A new car at twenty-six. {=26}
[a05] Holidays on credit cards.
[a06] A bigger apartment every time she gets a raise.
[a07] Her thirties look amazing online.
[a08] But Yara never builds a safety net.
[a09] When she loses her job at forty-four, the card balances pile up fast. {yara.job_loss_age}
[a10] At sixty-seven, Yara is still working. {yara.still_working_age}
[a11] Not because she wants to, but because she has to.
[a12] Frank forgot to live. Yara forgot that she'd keep living.

## bea
[b01] And then there's Bea.
[b02] Bea earns exactly the same as the other two. {=2}
[b03] But Bea has a system.
[b04] Step one: foundations first. {=1}
[b05] Before anything fun, Bea builds an emergency fund, clears high-interest debt, and sets up automatic retirement saving.
[b06] Those don't move.
[b07] Step two: she works out her 'enough' number, a rough target for what she'll need in retirement. {=2}
[b08] Once she's on track, extra saving isn't a must. It's a choice.
[b09] Step three: a guilt-free life fund. {=3}
[b10] Every month, a set amount goes into a pot just for experiences.
[b11] Concerts, trips, hobbies.
[b12] When she spends it, she doesn't feel guilty, because she planned it.
[b13] Step four: she plans by decade. {=4}
[b14] Bea writes down what she wants to do in her thirties, forties and fifties, and funds the things that expire first.
[b15] Backpacking at thirty-four. {=34}
[b16] Theme parks while her kids still want to go with her.
[b17] Step five: a yearly check-in. {=5}
[b18] If she's ahead of plan, she spends some of the extra on purpose.
[b19] If she's behind, the life fund shrinks for a while.

## end

[e05] And she finally went to Italy.
